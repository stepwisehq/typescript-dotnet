# Data-type completion campaign mandate

User prompt, preserved verbatim:

Great, let's commit work and continue:

Change the working model for the next phase: own a larger completion campaign, with internal milestones that you manage autonomously. I do not need to approve each feature slice.

The campaign should establish a coherent, usable object-type-checking capability, building on the completed scalar and anonymous-object work. Your recommended named-type work is a starting point, not a prescribed implementation sequence.

Retain all established requirements: Astra coordination, GPT-6.1 Sol subagents, close upstream fidelity, direct source-file mapping, clean and readable C#, appropriate .NET libraries, xUnit, and mandatory Native AOT verification.

Before extending the code, preserve the verified object milestone in a clean, reviewable checkpoint, without including unrelated changes.

**Define completion**

Inspect the relevant upstream implementation and establish a written completion contract for this campaign. Define the supported capability, necessary dependencies, observable acceptance criteria, and external boundaries.

Choose a target substantial enough to reduce repeated setup and handoffs. Distinguish completing the agreed capability from completing an entire upstream subsystem. Any excluded behavior that materially limits ordinary use must be prominent.

Share the contract in a concise progress update and proceed without waiting for approval unless a genuine scope or access decision requires my input. Do not later narrow the contract merely to declare success.

**Own the execution**

Determine and revise the internal milestones, implementation order, delegation, and verification strategy yourself. Move directly between internal milestones when their gates pass.

You may implement the checker dependencies necessary to meet the contract. Broad project infrastructure, module resolution, emit, incremental compilation, and language services remain outside this campaign. Escalate if the target requires a material expansion into those areas.

Improve efficiency based on observed work. Reduce repeated exploration, unnecessary context transfer, overlapping edits, and redundant validation. Retain meaningful regression protection and independent review. Choose validation frequency according to the risks of each change, with comprehensive campaign acceptance at completion.

Avoid building a process framework whose maintenance costs more than it saves.

**Establish defensible acceptance**

Maintain a clear inventory of the agreed behavior and its implementation and verification status. Test totals alone are not a completeness measure.

Have independent review assess both correctness and omissions against the completion contract and relevant upstream paths. Include interactions, recursive behavior where applicable, failures, and identity/cache behavior that the supported functionality depends on.

Keep unsupported paths explicit. Do not introduce permissive placeholders, fixture-specific implementations, or weakened comparisons. Keep missing-library diagnostics separately accounted for.

Actual Native AOT execution and agreement with pinned upstream behavior remain mandatory acceptance gates. Preserve scanner, parser, binder, and earlier checker regressions.

**Finish and report**

Continue through internal milestones until the contract is met or a material blocker requires a decision. Keep progress updates concise; do not turn each checkpoint into a request for another assignment.

At completion, provide the committed implementation, reproducible verification, a contract-based acceptance assessment, remaining limitations, and observed effort. Explain what can now be used reliably and recommend the next substantial campaign.

If the contract cannot be met within the established boundary, report the specific dependency or obstacle and the available choices. Do not relabel a partial result as subsystem completion.
